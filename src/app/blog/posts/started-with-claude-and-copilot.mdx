## Why I am writing this

I want to build a development process that is faster, clearer, and more consistent. I already have two tools installed and ready for that: Copilot as an agentic AI inside VS Code, and Claude for chat and research.

This post is my first step toward using them intentionally instead of casually. My goal is not to let AI do the work for me. My goal is to use it well enough that I can move with more confidence, spend less time stuck, and keep more energy for the parts of development that actually need judgment.

---

## The hard part is often starting

The biggest thing I struggle with is not writing code once I know what to build. It is figuring out what I should build in the first place.

Sometimes I sit down with a lot of energy, but the ideas feel too broad, too repetitive, or too uncertain to turn into anything useful. Other times I know I want to create something, but I cannot tell whether the idea is actually worth spending time on. That is where I usually lose momentum.

This is one reason I want Claude and Copilot in the same process. Claude can help me think through possibilities, narrow them down, and turn vague ideas into something concrete. Copilot can then help me move once the direction is clear. Together, they should make it easier to go from "I want to make something" to "I know what I am building." 

---

## Why these two tools

I do not see Copilot and Claude as competitors. I see them as different parts of the same workflow.

Copilot is best for the work that lives close to the codebase. When I need to inspect files, make edits, follow existing patterns, or move through an implementation step by step, Copilot fits naturally inside the editor.

Claude is better for the thinking side of the process. When I want to explore an idea, compare approaches, understand a concept, or talk through tradeoffs before touching the code, Claude gives me a good space for that.

That separation matters to me. I do not want one tool trying to do everything. I want each tool to do the kind of thinking it is best at.

---

## How I want to use Copilot

Copilot will be my hands-on assistant while I am working in VS Code.

I want to use it for things like:

- reading and understanding the existing code around a feature
- making focused edits instead of broad rewrites
- generating small utilities, components, or refactors
- helping me keep naming, structure, and style consistent
- moving quickly through repetitive code tasks

What I like about this kind of workflow is that it keeps me close to the code. I am still the one deciding what should change and why. Copilot just helps me move from intention to implementation faster.

I also want to be careful not to accept every suggestion automatically. Good output still needs review. If I do not understand a change, I should not keep it just because it looks convenient.

---

## How I want to use Claude

Claude will be the place where I slow down and think.

I want to use it for:

- research before starting a feature
- comparing different architecture choices
- planning a task before editing anything
- getting feedback on a draft idea or implementation approach
- checking whether I am missing a better path

This is useful because a lot of development problems are not really coding problems at first. They are clarity problems. If I do not understand the goal, the constraints, or the tradeoffs, then I can waste a lot of time writing code in the wrong direction.

Claude helps me reduce that risk. It gives me a place to ask questions, test assumptions, and get a broader view before I commit to a decision.

---

## The workflow I am aiming for

What I want is a simple loop:

1. Use Claude to understand the problem and narrow the options.
2. Use Copilot to make the actual changes in the codebase.
3. Check the result carefully instead of trusting the first pass.
4. Repeat until the feature, fix, or refactor is actually solid.

That workflow feels realistic to me because it separates exploration from execution. Claude helps me think better. Copilot helps me build faster. I still stay responsible for the quality of the result.

I think that is the right balance.

---

## What I expect to improve

If I use both tools well, I expect a few things to get better over time.

First, I should be able to start faster. Instead of spending too much time staring at a blank screen, I can ask for structure and move directly into work.

Second, I should make fewer avoidable mistakes. Researching first should help me avoid wrong assumptions and messy implementation decisions.

Third, I should become better at writing code I can explain. If I have to articulate the problem to Claude and then review Copilot's changes, I will be forced to think more clearly about the work itself.

That last part matters most to me. AI tools are useful, but the real skill is still judgment.

---

## Where I still need discipline

The biggest risk with AI tools is overreliance.

It is easy to let the tool keep talking while my own understanding gets weaker. It is also easy to mistake speed for progress. A fast answer is not the same thing as a correct one.

So I want a few rules for myself:

- I will not skip understanding just because the answer sounds confident.
- I will verify code changes before I trust them.
- I will use AI to support decisions, not replace them.
- I will still try to solve smaller problems on my own first when it makes sense.

I think this discipline is what will make the tools actually helpful in the long run.

---

## Closing thoughts

I am starting this workflow with a simple idea: use the right tool for the right kind of thinking.

Claude helps me reason.
Copilot helps me build.
I still have to do the deciding.

If I can keep that balance, I think I will be able to work faster without losing the quality of my thinking. And that is exactly the kind of development process I want to grow into.
